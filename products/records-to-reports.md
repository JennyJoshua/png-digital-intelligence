# Records-to-Reports

**Product ID:** PROD-001  
**Status:** Validation / pilot offer  
**Purpose:** Turn a small organisation's existing spreadsheet or register into a clear, checked monthly management report and a reusable, organised workbook.

## Customer problem

Small organisations often keep useful records in notebooks, spreadsheets, PDFs or several separate files. Preparing a reliable monthly summary can take time, and managers may struggle to see totals, outstanding items, unusual entries or trends.

This service organises customer-supplied records and produces a practical report. It is a records-processing and reporting service, not an audit, accounting certification or guarantee that the source records are correct.

## Initial target customers

Start with organisations that already maintain basic records but lack time or skills to summarise them:

- Small businesses and community organisations.
- Schools and training providers.
- Churches and NGOs.
- Small contractors, associations and project teams.

The first pilot should be with one organisation and one clearly defined record type. Do not initially promise to handle every kind of organisational data.

If the records exceed the agreed limits or require substantial manual reconstruction, pause and quote a separate scope before doing the extra work.

## Deliverables

1. **Clean workbook** with:
   - A preserved copy of the received source data where practical.
   - A cleaned and standardised table.
   - A summary sheet with key totals and counts.
   - A notes/issues sheet showing unresolved gaps, assumptions and items requiring customer confirmation.
2. **PDF management summary** with:
   - Reporting period and scope.
   - Key totals and indicators.
   - Important observations.
   - Data limitations and unresolved questions.
   - Recommended next actions.
3. **Handover note** explaining the files, calculations and any assumptions.

The report must distinguish verified calculations from customer-confirmed facts and from observations that need follow-up.

## Service workflow

1. **Enquiry and fit check:** establish the record type, purpose, approximate volume and desired output.
2. **Scope and quote:** agree the period, input files, row limit, outputs, exclusions, price and delivery date in writing.
3. **Data permission:** obtain confirmation that the customer is authorised to share the records and that the intended processing is lawful.
4. **Secure receipt:** use an agreed access-controlled transfer method. Do not ask customers to place confidential records in a public GitHub repository.
5. **Intake check:** confirm readability, completeness, fields, date range and obvious risks. Request clarification before processing where needed.
6. **Processing:** preserve source values, document transformations, deduplicate only under an agreed rule, and flag uncertain records rather than silently changing them.
7. **Quality checks:** reconcile row counts and totals, test formulas, sample-check transformations and confirm report figures match the workbook.
8. **Customer review:** provide draft outputs for one consolidated correction round.
9. **Final handover:** deliver final files and explain limitations.
10. **Retention and deletion:** agree a short retention period in advance; securely delete working copies when the agreed period ends, subject to legal obligations.

## Data protection rules

- Collect only the data needed for the agreed job.
- Do not use real customer records in public demos, sample repositories, AI prompts or unrelated projects.
- Avoid collecting sensitive personal information. If it is not essential, ask the customer to remove or mask it before transfer.
- Restrict access to the working files; do not use a public repository or public link for customer data.
- Do not promise security controls that have not been implemented and tested.
- Keep a record of the agreed scope, permission, transfer method and deletion date.
- Stop work and notify the customer if data appears to have been shared without authority or contains unexpected sensitive information.

## Pricing approach

K500 is an initial test price, not an established PNG market rate. The final price should reflect volume, data condition, manual effort, urgency, complexity and customer value. After the first few enquiries, compare estimated delivery hours with the fee and revise the scope or price.

Do not offer unlimited revisions or open-ended data cleaning at the pilot price.

## Success measures

A pilot is successful when:

- The customer receives all agreed outputs by the agreed date.
- Key calculations reconcile to the cleaned source data.
- The customer can understand and use the report without extensive explanation.
- Rework stays within the one agreed correction round.
- The actual effort and costs leave a commercially worthwhile margin.
- The customer confirms whether they would pay for another cycle or refer another organisation.
