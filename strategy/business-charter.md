# Business Charter — PNG Digital Intelligence

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Proposed venture charter  
**Date:** 18 November 2026

## 1. Purpose

To help organisations in Papua New Guinea convert scattered information, manual records and institutional knowledge into reliable, organised and useful digital intelligence.

## 2. Vision

A PNG where organisations can make better decisions and deliver better services because their information is accessible, structured and understood.

## 3. Mission

Deliver practical data, research, reporting, automation and knowledge-management services that solve real organisational problems at a scale appropriate to PNG customers.

## 4. Positioning

**Turning scattered information into valuable intelligence.**

The venture will not position itself as a generic software house, accounting platform, business directory or general-purpose AI chatbot. Its differentiation should be the quality and usefulness of the underlying information, sector understanding, source traceability and practical delivery.

## 5. Initial service divisions

### A. Data intelligence and research
- Data collection from authorised public and client-provided sources.
- Data cleaning, deduplication, classification and structuring.
- Sector-specific datasets and research briefs.
- Source verification, metadata and update tracking.

### B. Business automation and reporting
- Spreadsheet, CSV, PDF and database data conversion.
- Records-to-reports services.
- Recurring operational and management reports.
- Lightweight workflow automation where it reduces real manual work.

### C. Digital knowledge infrastructure
- Document and archive inventory and preparation.
- OCR, extraction, classification and metadata.
- Searchable knowledge collections and RAG-ready source packages.
- Maintenance and update services for knowledge collections.

## 6. Target customers

Potential customer groups include SMEs, schools, vocational institutions, NGOs, development organisations, churches, contractors and suppliers, consultants, researchers and public institutions.

These are target segments for discovery, not confirmed customers.

## 7. Commercial objectives

1. Secure an initial paid pilot with a clearly bounded deliverable.
2. Identify one repeatable service with demonstrable customer value.
3. Convert suitable one-off projects into recurring maintenance or reporting relationships.
4. Build reusable assets that can be licensed or sold repeatedly where rights permit.
5. Expand only after delivery quality, customer demand and unit economics are understood.

## 8. Operating principles

- Evidence before product development.
- Human verification for consequential data and research.
- Clear source attribution and update dates.
- Respect privacy, confidentiality, copyright, database rights and website terms.
- No unauthorised scraping, republishing or resale of third-party datasets.
- Keep client data out of public repositories; use appropriate access-controlled storage.
- Be transparent about coverage gaps, estimates and uncertainty.
- Use free and low-cost infrastructure where practical.

## 9. Success measures

Early measures should include:
- Number of customer discovery conversations.
- Number of qualified problems and pilot proposals.
- Paid pilots completed and customer acceptance.
- Delivery time and direct delivery cost.
- Repeat purchase and subscription conversion.
- Data quality issues, correction rate and freshness.
- Gross margin per service.

Do not use downloads, website visits or dataset volume as substitutes for paying demand.

## 10. Scope boundaries

The venture will not initially:
- Build a general accounting or payroll product.
- Duplicate existing business directories or supplier portals.
- Compete directly with large-scale field research agencies.
- Sell personal, confidential or unlawfully obtained information.
- Promise that AI-generated output is authoritative without source verification.
- Build a large platform before validating a narrow paid use case.

## 11. Review

Review this charter after the first customer discovery and pilot cycle. Material changes to target market, business model or service scope should be recorded as decisions in GitHub Issues.
