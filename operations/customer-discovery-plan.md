# Customer Discovery and First-Sale Validation

**Related product:** [Records-to-Reports](../products/records-to-reports.md)  
**Status:** Ready for controlled validation; no customer outreach has been performed by this document.

## Objective

Find out whether PNG organisations have a recurring, costly problem preparing reports from existing records—and whether at least one will pay for a tightly scoped pilot.

The objective is not to collect compliments or general interest. Evidence of a real problem, current workaround, decision-maker, budget and willingness to pay matters more.

## Initial customer segment

Begin with small organisations in reachable networks, but do not assume that personal familiarity means they are suitable customers. Prioritise organisations that:

- Maintain records in Excel, notebooks, PDFs or multiple files.
- Prepare monthly or periodic reports manually.
- Have a manager or owner who uses the reports to make decisions.
- Can provide a small, authorised, non-sensitive sample or anonymised description.
- Can approve and pay for a small project.

Potential segments to test: small businesses, schools/training providers, churches, NGOs and community organisations. Do not approach all segments at once; start with one segment where access to a decision-maker is easiest.

## Discovery target

Conduct **10 short conversations** across no more than two initial segments. Ten is a learning target, not proof of the whole PNG market.

Suggested mix:
- 5 small businesses or contractors.
- 5 schools, NGOs, churches or training providers.

If one segment produces substantially stronger evidence, focus the next round there.

## Interview guide

Ask about past behaviour rather than pitching first:

1. How do you currently keep the records needed for your monthly or periodic report?
2. Who prepares the report, and how long does it normally take?
3. What usually makes the process difficult?
4. What errors, delays or missing information have caused problems recently?
5. What do managers or owners need to understand from the report?
6. What do you currently do when records are spread across different files or notebooks?
7. Have you paid anyone to organise records or prepare reports before? What did you pay for?
8. Who decides whether to purchase this kind of help, and what approval is needed?
9. What would make you trust an external provider with the work?
10. If a small one-period pilot solved the problem, what would need to be included for it to be worth paying for?

Only after understanding the problem, describe the proposed K500 pilot and ask:
- Is the scope relevant to a real report you need?
- Would you authorise a paid pilot at that price, subject to a written scope and data-handling agreement?
- If not, what specifically prevents it: price, trust, timing, data readiness, authority or lack of need?

Do not pressure interviewees or imply that a purchase is expected.

## Evidence log

Record only business-relevant information and avoid unnecessary personal details.

| Field | What to capture |
|---|---|
| Interview ID | INT-01, INT-02, etc. |
| Segment | Business, school, NGO, church, contractor, other |
| Role | Decision-maker, report preparer, user, other |
| Current process | Tools and steps used |
| Pain | Specific recent problem |
| Frequency | Weekly, monthly, quarterly, occasional |
| Impact | Time, delay, errors, missed decisions or other consequence |
| Existing spend | Whether they currently pay for a workaround |
| Buying authority | Who can approve |
| Trust/data concerns | Their stated requirements |
| Price response | Exact response to the K500 pilot hypothesis |
| Next step | None, follow-up, sample review, quote requested |
| Evidence strength | Strong, medium or weak, with reason |

Do not put customer records or personal data in this public repository. Store interview notes here only if they are genuinely non-confidential and the interviewee has agreed; otherwise keep them in appropriately access-controlled storage and record only an anonymised summary.

## Validation thresholds

After 10 conversations, assess the evidence:

**Continue to a paid pilot if all are true:**
- At least 6 of 10 describe a recurring reporting/records problem with a concrete example.
- At least 4 can identify a decision-maker and a realistic purchasing route.
- At least 2 request a quote, sample or next meeting after hearing the offer.
- At least 1 customer agrees to a paid pilot at K500 or a mutually agreed price and scope.

**Revise the offer if:**
- The problem is real but the desired output differs (for example, cash summary, stock report, attendance report or project expenditure report).
- Customers want a smaller scope or need a different price.
- Trust, privacy or data-readiness concerns prevent a pilot.

**Pause or change segment if:**
- Fewer than 4 of 10 describe a recurring problem.
- No one can identify a budget owner or purchasing route.
- Interest is polite but nobody takes a concrete next step.

These are practical decision thresholds for this first experiment, not statistically representative market research.

## First-sale sequence

1. Complete the discovery conversations.
2. Select one common record type and one customer problem.
3. Prepare a short anonymised sample relevant to that segment.
4. Offer a written, fixed-scope pilot with price, limits, delivery date, revision and data-handling terms.
5. Obtain written approval and any required deposit/payment arrangement before processing real data.
6. Receive only the minimum authorised data through an access-controlled method.
7. Deliver, request feedback and measure actual hours and rework.
8. Ask for a testimonial or referral only with explicit permission.

## Do not do yet

- Do not build a custom application before validating the service.
- Do not buy software, APIs, hosting or subscriptions for this experiment.
- Do not scrape or resell third-party datasets.
- Do not promise accounting, audit, legal or regulatory certification.
- Do not use school or employer records without the institution's formal authorisation.
- Do not process confidential or sensitive records until an appropriate secure workflow and written terms are in place.

## Decision record

At the end of the discovery round, record:
- Number of conversations completed.
- Most common customer segment and problem.
- Evidence of current spending or costly workarounds.
- Number of quote requests and paid-pilot commitments.
- Reasons for rejection.
- Recommended next action: proceed, revise, change segment or stop.
